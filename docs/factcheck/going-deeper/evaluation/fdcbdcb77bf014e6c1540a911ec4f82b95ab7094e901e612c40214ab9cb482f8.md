## Emanuel's take, in full

> I trust a tool more once it's been tested. I trust it more again when I know where the test stopped. A test is a torch. It lights the spot you point it at. Everything else stays dark.

> So a good report says two things. Here is what we measured. Here is what we didn't. Which part do I read first? The second. That's where the surprises are.

> Then I ask who was in the test, and who was missing. **Measure it. Then say what you don't know.** Keeping a tool, fixing it or switching it off is a human decision. Honest limits make that decision possible.
