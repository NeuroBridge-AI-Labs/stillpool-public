## Emanuel's take, in full

> A chatbot can sound certain and be wrong. You've just seen why. So should we stop trusting it? I don't think so.

> I think of it like a map. A map is useful, and a map can be out of date. So you look up and check the road. That's all I'm asking for here. **Informed trust.** Blind faith hands your judgement away. Total rejection throws a useful tool away. Both are easier than thinking, and both cost you.

> Understanding sits between them. Know how the answer was made. Know where it tends to slip. Then check what matters. **Trust that has been checked is worth having.**

## Facts in this change

- The lesson has shown why this happens: PASS, index.mdx:151-169 explains generation, training objectives and evaluation incentives.
- Where it slips can be checked against sources: PASS, index.mdx:229-250 gives fabricated citations and the duty to verify court filings; the take does not promise that all errors can be found.
- A map can become out of date: ordinary map analogy, not an empirical claim about a model's live data access.

## Opinion boundary
